# Cuboid Studio — Demo Voiceover (EN)

Audience: professional architects. Target: ~5 min at a measured pace (~520 words).
`[SITE CONTEXT]` slots are filled per run. `[SCREEN]` cues describe the capture underneath.
Network-dependent beats are marked ⚡ — in a live/offline setting, run them from a restored saved state.

---

**0:00 — Opening**
[SCREEN: Map tab, empty map]

Every design tool you work with begins from the same substrate: property lines, setbacks, sun angles. Facts. What none of them carry is the conversation a place is already having about itself — the frustrations, the jokes, the shared readings that circulate long before an architect arrives. Cuboid Studio is built on a claim: that conversation is design input, and its translation into geometry can be made measurable and inspectable rather than metaphorical.

**0:30 — Step 1: Site** ⚡
[SCREEN: typing the address, radius selector, POI layers appearing]

We start with a site. [SITE CONTEXT — one or two sentences on the chosen site.] The app geocodes the address and pulls the surrounding fabric — transit, education, civic uses, green space, markets — into a structured site context. The decision here: this context is not a backdrop. It's written into a persistent site record that every later step reads. The cultural translation you'll see in a moment is site-specific by construction — the same meme cuts differently at [SITE] than anywhere else.

**1:15 — Step 2: Encode** ⚡
[SCREEN: photo upload, five-axis reading panel appearing before any geometry]

Next, photographs of an inhabited space — one primary, up to six supplementary. Before the system commits to any geometry, it publishes its reading of the space on five explicit axes: atmosphere, light, and emotion as continuous scales; rhythm and placement as categories. This is deliberate. The reading is a typed intermediate representation — you can inspect it, contest it, and edit it, and the system preserves both the model's original and your revision. If you disagree with the machine's judgment, you overrule it before a single cube is placed. Only then does the reading propose an assembly.

**2:15 — Step 3: The vocabulary**
[SCREEN: Builder, variation picker, placing cubes]

Everything is built from one vocabulary: seventy cube variations, each produced by choosing four of eight fixed cutters — five spheres, three cylinders — subtracted from the same base cube. Eight primitives, no more. Because the vocabulary is finite and fixed, every relationship is unambiguous: two cubes either share a cutter or they don't, and connection follows from that. And when you save, the system stores the cutting conditions, not the mesh — the form regenerates from its forces every time it loads.

**3:00 — Step 4: Pataphysical translation** ⚡
[SCREEN: Evolution → Pataphysical, meme browser, two-pass result panel]

Now the cultural material. From the meme library — collected and annotated on the archthesis platform — pick one. The translation runs in two passes. First, cultural extraction: what rhetorical move the meme makes, what tension powers it, how it resonates with this specific site. Second, geometric translation: a named operator — erosion, consolidation, amplification, inversion — with explicit targets, magnitude and decay, its reasoning, and a four-axis confidence vector. The system tells you where its own translation is weak. You can tweak parameters before applying. Nothing between the joke and the cut is a black box.

**4:00 — Step 5: Evolve** ⚡ (generation) / scoring is client-side
[SCREEN: candidate list, amber preview highlight, compressibility sparkline]

Evolve generates a pool of candidate translations and scores each one — not against an optimum, but by compression progress: how much more structured, more learnable, the assembly became. Four measurable sub-scores: geometric clustering, spatial regularity, operator repetition, meme coherence. Candidates arrive ranked; you preview, apply, or discard. The machine proposes; the architect curates.

**4:40 — Step 6: Decode**
[SCREEN: Decode canvas, placing tile glyphs, SVG/DXF export]

Finally, Decode: the assembly re-enters architectural convention as two-dimensional notation. Each variation has a glyph; you place, rotate, snap to grid — and export SVG or DXF straight into your documentation pipeline. This step is manual by design: decoding is authorship too.

**5:10 — Close**
[SCREEN: slow orbit of the finished assembly]

At no point did the system replace judgment. Every automated step published an intermediate you could read, contest, and revise. That is the position: the architect as curatorial translator — and culture, finally, as a first-class design material.
